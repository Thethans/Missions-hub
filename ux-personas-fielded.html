Fielded · UX Research

Field Personas

Three composite personas covering Fielded's primary user segments — discernment, comparison, and pre-field logistics — drawn from the site's actual surfaces: the agency-match quiz, the opportunities explorer, and the Supabase-backed checklist.

Segment 01 First-time seeker
MR

Maya Reyes

23 · Nursing student

Location
Wichita, KS
Stage
Discernment
Sending context
Campus ministry
Found via
Instagram bio link
Tech proficiency

"I don't want to sign up for anything — I just want to know if this is even for me."

Goals

PrimaryFind out whether missions is a realistic path before telling anyone she's considering it.

SecondarySee which agencies would even want someone with her skill set (pre-licensure nursing).

Pain points

  • Doesn't know the vocabulary — "UPG," "sending agency," "member care" all read as jargon.
  • Assumes any quiz result is a lead-gen funnel in disguise.
  • 1,500 listings feels like a wall, not a starting point.

Behaviors

  • Googles "how to become a missionary" late at night.
  • Follows missionary accounts on Instagram; hasn't contacted an agency directly yet.
  • Screenshots things to ask her small-group leader about later.

Motivations

  • Wants honesty over polish — a plain "we don't know" beats a confident guess.
  • Needs to feel she can leave without having "committed" to anything.

Scenario

Maya opens the quiz link during a study break, phone in hand, expecting a sales pitch after three questions. When an agency's support-cost field shows "worth asking about" instead of a confident number, she relaxes a little — it doesn't feel like it's trying to close her. She saves three matches to revisit after finals and doesn't create an account.

Segment 02 Comparison shopper
DO

Dana Okafor

34 · Former TESOL teacher

Location
Austin, TX
Stage
Active applicant, 1 agency
Household
Spouse + 2 kids
Found via
Google, "TESOL missions agencies"
Tech proficiency

"Show me what's actually different — not just what every agency says about itself."

Goals

PrimaryCompare placement types, field support, and cost structure across 2–3 agencies against the one she's already applying to.

SecondaryFind education-sector openings in regions viable for a family with school-age kids.

Pain points

  • Distrusts self-reported agency copy — "everyone says they have great member care."
  • Generic listings waste her time; she wants filters that actually narrow the set.
  • Family logistics (schooling, healthcare abroad) rarely show up as filterable fields.

Behaviors

  • Keeps a spreadsheet scoring agencies on her own criteria.
  • Reads missionary-alumni forums for the parts agencies don't put in brochures.
  • Emails agencies directly with pointed, specific questions.

Motivations

  • Efficiency — she's done the emotional discernment; now it's due diligence.
  • Wants tradeoffs named plainly, not smoothed over.

Scenario

Dana skips the quiz — she's past that stage — and goes straight to the opportunities explorer on her laptop. She filters by region, "family-friendly," and education sector, opens inquiry forms on two listings that clear her bar, and saves the rest to cross-check against her spreadsheet that evening. A filter that silently returns zero results without explaining why is the fastest way to lose her.

Segment 03 Pre-field / returning user
GL

Grace Lindqvist

41 · Nurse practitioner

Location
Minneapolis, MN
Stage
Accepted, 4 months to deployment
Sending context
Home church support team
Found via
Her agency's onboarding email
Tech proficiency

"I just need this thing to remember where I left off."

Goals

PrimaryWork through pre-field logistics — training, paperwork, support raising — without losing progress between sessions.

SecondaryKeep her home church's prayer team updated without a separate tool.

Pain points

  • A past support-raising tool logged her out mid-form and lost an afternoon's work.
  • Anxious about missing a required step with no visible way to confirm it's done.
  • Gets flustered by auth flows that don't clearly confirm success or failure.

Behaviors

  • Checks the checklist on a set weekly cadence, usually Tuesday mornings.
  • Signs in by magic link from whatever device is nearest.
  • Posts short prayer updates for her sending church after each session.

Motivations

  • Reliability over features — she will not forgive lost progress twice.
  • Wants to feel accompanied, not just administered to.

Scenario

Grace logs in via a magic-link email between patients. The checklist opens exactly where she left it — "Medical clearance" still unchecked from last week. She uploads confirmation, checks it off, watches for the save to register, then posts a two-line update to the prayer map before her next appointment. If the check mark doesn't visibly stick, she'll email her agency contact to ask if it actually went through.

Composite personas for internal UX evaluation — not drawn from a fielded research study. Built to stress-test onboarding, filtering, and checklist/auth flows from three distinct entry points into the site.
